What it draws. A central ring — the stage — with six lines running out to six terminals. Six for the sixth edition, and every line is the same length and angle, so the mark is perfectly radial: rotate it any multiple of 60° and nothing changes.
Why it reads as music, not tech. Signal flow, not circuitry: the lines leave the centre and stop at open dots, like output rather than wiring. No connectors, no right angles, no chips.
Where it wins. A circle is already the shape of every profile picture, so no crop is ever wrong. Embroiders in one thread colour; cuts as vinyl for a standee; needs no background to work.
The catch. Radial marks are quiet — they sit still. If you want the brand to feel like motion, 4b does that better.

What it draws. A vertical axis with six arcs — three left, three right — stepping outward at even 14-unit intervals. Sound leaving a source in both directions, six waves for the sixth edition, and the axis reads as the two nights either side of one event.
Why it reads as music, not tech. Arcs are the universal shorthand for sound, but here they are symmetrical rather than the usual one-sided wifi fan — closer to a plan view of a room filling up than a signal icon.
Where it wins. It has direction and rhythm; on a tall standee or a hoodie back it feels like it's moving outward. At small sizes it thins to one arc pair pushed outward from the axis, so the gaps stay open at 16px — same mark, fewer strokes.
The catch. More strokes than 4a, so it needs a little more clear space, and screen-printing on dark fabric wants the 24px version's heavier stroke.
